WHAT THE PAPER ACTUALLY ARGUES
That high-performance GEMM is not a matter of clever inner loops but of a LAYERED
DECOMPOSITION chosen so that the innermost kernels can keep their operands in the right
level of the memory hierarchy. Performance is treated as a consequence of data placement,
and the loop structure is derived from that constraint rather than tuned toward it.

The decomposition (paper's own naming, §3):

    gemm  ->  gepp | gemp | gepm        (panel-panel, matrix-panel, panel-matrix)
              ->  gebp | gepb | gepdot  (block-panel, panel-block, dot)

The claim is that if the three lowest kernels run at near-peak, every higher case inherits
it — so the whole optimisation problem reduces to gebp/gepb/gepdot.

THE PART THAT GENERALISES BEYOND BLAS (§4.2, §6)
  - Choose WHICH CACHE LEVEL the blocked operand lives in, deliberately. The paper argues
    for the layer furthest from the registers that still satisfies its assumptions, because
    a larger mc x nc amortises the RAM<->cache movement over more arithmetic. L1 is the
    obvious-looking choice and the wrong one.
  - PACK operands into contiguous buffers before the kernel runs. The copy is not overhead
    to be eliminated; it buys unit-stride access and TLB behaviour that pay for it many
    times over. This is the counter-intuitive result most people take from the paper.
  - Register blocking (mr x nr) is chosen against register file size, and kc and mc against
    cache capacity and TLB reach — each parameter tied to a named hardware resource rather
    than found by search.
  - Assumptions (a)-(c) are stated explicitly and the derivation depends on them. The paper
    is unusually careful to say when its own model stops holding.

WHY IT IS IN THIS CORPUS
Determinex's AMD Radeon claim is that GPU batch throughput converts into correctness:
P(solve) = 1-(1-p)^K, so accuracy is bounded by how fast independent attempts arrive. That
makes throughput a first-class correctness concern rather than a performance footnote, and
this paper is the canonical statement of how throughput is actually obtained on real memory
hierarchies — by arranging data movement, not by counting FLOPs. The prefix-cache result in
the submission (93.5% hits shared vs ~1% distinct, measured on the GPU) is the same shape of
argument one level up: the access PATTERN is the optimisation.
