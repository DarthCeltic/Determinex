Relative Representations Enable Zero-Shot Latent Space Communication
Luca Moschella, Valentino Maiorca, Marco Fumero, Antonio Norelli, Francesco Locatello, Emanuele Rodola
ICLR 2023 (Oral, top 5%)
arXiv: 2209.15430

Abstract:
Latent representations are a cornerstone of deep learning, yet they remain largely opaque:
two models trained on the same data with the same architecture yield vectors that are not
directly comparable. We show that neural representations are not arbitrarily different —
they preserve angular structure. If you express each data point as its cosine similarity
to a set of anchor points, the resulting relative representations are invariant to latent
isometries and rescalings. Two models trained on the same data will produce isomorphic
relative representations, enabling zero-shot model stitching: composing the encoder of
one model with the decoder of another without any joint training. We validate this across
images, text, and graphs using CNNs, GCNs, and Transformers.

Key contributions:
- Relative representation: encode data as similarity to fixed anchor set rather than absolute coordinates
- Latent isometry invariance: same angular structure regardless of random initialization
- Zero-shot model stitching across architectures trained on the same distribution
- Validated on image classification, NLP tasks, graph tasks
- Platonic Representation Hypothesis: models converge to geometrically similar representations

Relationship to Determinex Rosetta Stone:
Determinex's Rosetta Stone draws on this theoretical framework but uses trained MLP encoder/decoder
pairs (not analytical cosine-to-anchors) to bridge heterogeneous models with different
embedding dimensionalities and different pretraining corpora. The Rosetta Stone is a trained
artifact (rosetta_v1.pt) requiring InfoNCE contrastive training; Moschella et al. is training-free.
